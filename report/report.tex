\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We compute how a gravitational wave (GW) from a compact binary is lensed,
in wave optics, by a third, more massive body in the same hierarchical
triple, for the same triple at two epochs of the inner binary's inspiral: a
late chirp (lens frozen for the whole observation) and an earlier,
quasi-monochromatic phase (the outer orbit's motion is resolved, producing
periodic "repeated lensing"). All figures and numbers here are produced by
the scripts in \code{cases/}, checked against independent methods in
\code{tests/}, and derived from first principles in
\href{../theory/theory.pdf}{\code{theory/theory.pdf}}, which this document
does not repeat. This page/PDF is the results; the repository behind it
(\code{README.md}) is what makes them reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{The system}

One hierarchical triple (\code{src/gwlens/system.py}), followed at two
points in its own evolution:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
inner binary (the source) & $m_1=20\,\Msun$, $m_2=15\,\Msun$ ($\mathcal M=15.05\,\Msun$) \\
lens & $M_L=5\times10^4\,\Msun$, at $D_L=5$ kpc \\
outer orbit & $a_\mathrm{out}=1.82$ AU, $P_\mathrm{out}=4$ d, $i_\mathrm{out}=87^\circ$, circular \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The lens mass is chosen (not astrophysically typical) so that the
wave-optics parameter $w=8\pi GM_Lf/c^3$ is not extreme at either epoch's
frequency; the hierarchy $a_\mathrm{in}\ll a_\mathrm{out}$ holds by 2--6
orders of magnitude at both. Full justification: \code{theory/theory.pdf}
Ch.~1, \S6.1; regime checks: \code{tests/test\_system.py}.

\section{Case A --- the chirp, static lens}
\label{sec:caseA}

The inner binary inspirals from $f=10$~Hz to $f_\mathrm{isco}=125.6$~Hz in
\textbf{15.18 s} --- $4.4\times10^{-5}$ of the 4-day outer period, so the
lens sits at one impact parameter, $y_A=1.589$ (the phase of closest
projected approach during the outer orbit), for the entire chirp. The
unlensed waveform is the restricted TaylorF2 (2PN) frequency-domain
post-Newtonian inspiral --- the functional form used in real LIGO/Virgo
matched-filter search templates, not a hand-built approximation
(\code{src/gwlens/taylorf2.py}).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{../cases/case_A_chirp/caseA_F_of_f.png}
\caption{The amplification factor $F(f)$ across the whole chirp band (top)
and zoomed to 3 Hz (bottom), where the individual two-image interference

\end{figure}

\caption{The extended amplification pattern on the source plane: concentric

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{../cases/case_A_chirp/caseA_strain_time.png}
\caption{Lensed vs.\ unlensed time-domain chirp strain (arbitrary units,

Peak-sample amplification is $1.038$; RMS amplification (averaged over the
whole chirp) is $1.056$. These being close but not equal, despite $|F|$
ranging $0.79$--$1.27$ across the band, is itself informative: $F(f)$
modulates \emph{phase} as well as amplitude, so the lensed waveform is a
\emph{dephased} copy of the unlensed one, not simply a rescaled one --- a
genuinely wave-optics statement (in the geometric-optics limit, lensing
\emph{is} just a real magnification).

\section{Case B --- quasi-monochromatic, moving lens}
\label{sec:caseB}

Earlier in the same inspiral: $f=0.05$~Hz, drifting only $4\%$ over a
24-day (6 outer period) observation, with 60.2 outer periods still left
before merger --- quasi-monochromatic. $w_B=0.309$ throughout (fixed; only
the impact parameter $y(t)$ varies): squarely in the diffraction-dominated
regime.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{../cases/case_B_monochromatic/caseB_pattern_with_orbit.png}
\caption{The same kind of fixed diffraction pattern as Case A (here at the
much smaller $w_B=0.309$) with the source's actual sky-projected outer
-orbit track overlaid: cyan where the source is lensed (behind the lens
along the line of sight), dashed green where it is not (in front of it ---
see below).}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{../cases/case_B_monochromatic/caseB_repeated_pulses.png}
\caption{$|F(w_B,y(t))|^2$ over the full 24-day observation: six periodic
amplification pulses, one per outer period, peaking at $|F|^2=1.385$, with
diffraction ringing visible on both sides of each peak.}
\end{figure}

Because the lens and source are in the \emph{same} system rather than at
the usual well-separated cosmological distances, $D_{LS}(t)$ is set by the
outer orbit itself and vanishes twice per period; \textbf{exactly half the
orbit turns out to be unlensed} (source in front of the lens, not behind
it, so no lensing geometry exists there at all --- not an approximation,
$F=1$ by construction). This is the origin of the periodic pulses above,
the wave-optics analogue of the "repeated lensing" found in the
geometric-optics limit by \citet{DOrazioLoeb2020}. Full derivation:
\code{theory/theory.pdf} \S4.2--4.3 (which also quantifies exactly how far
this pushes the standard thin-lens approximation, and shows the effect is
confined to a $\sim$60 s window per crossing, coincident with the already
-trivial $F\approx1$ region --- not a concern for anything shown here).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{../cases/case_B_monochromatic/caseB_detector_view.png}
\caption{Idealized detector view. Top: strain envelope over the full
observation, six clean pulses riding on the slowly-drifting carrier.
Bottom: a fine time grid zoomed on one pulse peak, where the lensed and

\section{What the two cases show together}

The same lens, the same formalism, two very different-looking signatures,
governed entirely by one timescale ratio (merger duration vs.\ outer
period vs.\ observation length):

\begin{itemize}
  \item Case A: a single, frozen diffraction pattern sampled at one point,
    swept across in \emph{frequency} by the chirp itself --- dense
    interference fringes, checked to approach the geometric-optics limit
    as $w\to\infty$.
  \item Case B: the same kind of pattern, at fixed frequency, sampled in
    \emph{time} by the outer orbital motion --- periodic pulses, with
    exactly half the orbit unlensed by the triple's own geometry.
  \item Both cases: lensing is not simply "make it brighter." $F(f)$ (or
    $F(t)$) is complex, and its phase leaves a real, checkable mark on the
    waveform beyond an overall amplitude.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and limitations}

Stated explicitly, not left implicit (each also logged in \code{wiki/log.md}
at the point it was decided):
\begin{itemize}
  \item Point-mass lens only; no extended mass distribution.
  \item TaylorF2 truncated at 2PN (Case A); Case B's underlying frequency
    evolution is leading-order only.
  \item No absolute strain calibration (inclination/polarization
    prefactors folded into an $O(1)$ constant) --- only relative amplitudes
    and the lensing amplification factor are physical claims here.
  \item The idealized detector view has no noise, antenna pattern, or
    matched filtering --- illustrative only, as scoped from the start.
  \item The thin-lens/paraxial approximation is quantifiably pushed near
    each Case B front/back crossing (\S\ref{sec:caseB}); shown not to
    affect anything reported.
\end{itemize}
